What this picture is doing

Y-axis: what the LLM substitutes for

The chapters use seven colours; the colours encode what method the paper runs, not what the paper is modelling. The Y-axis here is the second question. Reading top-to-bottom: position papers about the field; populations of agents; aggregate effects across many people; one person or persona; and at the bottom, the LLM as a player whose own behaviour is the object of measurement.

The bands are not progress — a Week-2 yellow paper is not "behind" a Week-5 brown one. Each band asks a different question; the field's progression has been into more bands at once, not from one band to the next.

The three big lineages out of Park 2023

Smallville is the W4 paper that fans out into all three Week-5 papers. Project Sid scales the move up on two axes at once — more agents and a much longer simulated horizon. The horizon is what actually matters: Smallville's two days reach information diffusion and a Valentine's-day party; Project Sid's longer arc is where Durkheim-shaped phenomena (specialisation, cultural transmission, emergent governance) become observable. It also keeps an explicit cognitive architecture (PIANO) at a moment when individual prediction (Park 2024) had argued long context could replace architecture.

Ashery moves in the opposite direction — strips the agent down to almost nothing — and tests whether the macro mechanism (Centola 2018's naming-game tipping point) survives the strip-down. Larooij & Törnberg apply the move to platforms: a population becomes a policy testbed.

The horizontal substitution debate

Horton 2023 proposed silicon-sampling — anchor an LLM to a persona, run an experiment on it. Park 2024 is the strongest empirical case for that proposal, eighteen months later, with two-hour interviews replacing one-paragraph personas. The thick blue line is the silicon-sampling lineage carrying Week 2's programmatic move into Week 4's empirical defence.

The dashed line between Hewitt and Larooij is the alternative on the other side: instead of simulating the population, ask the LLM directly to forecast the experiment. Two methods that often answer the same policy question and can disagree.

The methodological top band

The three position-paper-shaped objects across W2–W4 read as a single conversation: Sun 2025 maps the field; Kozlowski & Evans 2025 issue a six-weakness validity critique; Anthis 2025 respond with a five-challenge optimistic-engineering programme. The top arrow is the conversation, not three independent surveys.

One absence worth naming: no Week-5 or Week-6 position paper. The empirical 🟫 and Week-6 RL papers have got ahead of the methodological commentary on them.

Where RL re-enters the picture (Week 6)

The bottom band gains three agents this week — Xu (LLM + RL on Werewolf), FAIR/CICERO (planner + LLM on Diplomacy), and Dolgopolov (pure Q-learning on a stateless Prisoner's Dilemma). The first two are direct responses to the strategic-bias signal Akata picked up in Week 2: prompt-only LLMs play strategically worse than humans, and adding an explicit optimizer on top of the LLM closes most of the gap. The dashed arrow from Akata across four columns to Xu is that response.

Dolgopolov sits at the opposite extreme — no LLM at all, a closed-form characterisation of when stateless Q-learners cooperate as a function of learning rate and payoff. The dashed arrow from Ashery down to Dolgopolov is the methodological move from simulated minimal-agent emergence to its analytical limit case.

McKee is the genuinely new shape — pink in the legend, in the Population band but with the agent acting on the population rather than living inside it. The arrow from Larooij reads as the cohort-relevant transition: from human-designed prosocial interventions (chronological feed, bridging-based ranking) to an RL agent that learns the intervention by playing against simulated humans and is validated on real ones. This is the Week-6 move that generalises furthest into the cohort's experimental-economics background.

RL as institution designer (Week 7)

Week 7 takes the pink McKee shape and presses it harder. The two papers — Koster, Tacchetti et al. 2024 and Koster, Pîslar et al. 2025 — keep the agent acting on the population, but widen its action space from rewiring a fixed network to choosing the payoff rule the population plays under. The first is the programmatic statement: deep RL as a general mechanism-design instrument — name a welfare target, simulate a population, search the rule space. The second points the same machinery at a common-pool resource game and reports the empirical performance/legibility frontier.

The arrow from McKee across the divider is the cleanest single trajectory in the syllabus: graph-rewiring planner (2023) → mechanism-design framework (2024) → learned redistribution rule with a measured legitimacy frontier (2025). The design question classical mechanism design settled in closed form has not vanished — it has moved into two choices the diagram cannot draw: which welfare target, and which simulated population.

The "missed-mixture" thread (Week-3 §8)

The blue dashed thread links three places where within-prompt distribution structure is reported but unanalysed — Lorè's bimodal GPT-4 in W2, Centaur's Fig. 2c bimodal trajectories in W3 (the partial counter-example), and Ashery's seed-dependent convention bimodality in W5.

The thread crosses three colours and four weeks; it is a research-question shape the existing colour scheme does not encode. A project that explicitly recovered behavioural types from any of these three datasets would close the gap between this literature and the behavioural-GT tradition that closed it for humans in 1996 (Fischbacher, Gächter & Fehr).

What the picture obscures (and why that's OK)

Two things the diagram drops on purpose: publication date (the X-axis is syllabus order, not chronology — Sun 2025 and Anthis 2025 were published in the same year despite being plotted three columns apart) and citation graph (only methodologically load-bearing connections are drawn; many additional citations exist).

The picture is built to surface the field's methodological geometry, not its bibliometric one. If the geometry is wrong, the picture is the thing to redraw.

Open questions about the visual

Things to push on before this becomes a chapter figure:

• Is the Y-axis the right cut? Two alternatives: validation rigor (none → external ground truth) or agent richness (minimal → persona-conditioned). Each ranks the papers differently.

• Should Centaur sit higher than the W2/W3 yellows and reds? It predicts individuals, not just measures the model — the current placement may understate it.

• Should Willis and Akata climb a band into "population"? Two-agent repeated play is closer to a population-level claim than a single-decision measurement.

• Lineage lines: the diagram shows eleven; about a dozen more would be defensible. Which would help vs. clutter?

• Is the bottom band's relabel from "the LLM itself" to "the agent itself" the right one? It now houses two LLM-hybrids (Xu, FAIR) and a pure RL learner (Dolgopolov) — the same band but a wider taxonomy of agents than the W2/W3 yellows and reds.

• McKee and the two Week-7 Koster papers are plotted in the Population band because their object of intervention is a population. But the agent in each is a single RL designer, not a member of the population — with three such papers now, the case for a dedicated "designer" band above Methodology is stronger than it was at Week 6. Is the current shared-band placement still defensible, or does the designer move deserve its own row?
